Public Docket research kit

A commons upgrade shipped beside the first roster — so accountability is not only oppositional. Print it, teach it, improve it.

1 · Label hygiene

  • Opinion — clearly marked as yours or a named commentator’s; never presented as court fact.
  • Allegation — attributed (“ICC warrant alleges…”, “filing claims…”); link the primary document.
  • Adjudicated / official fact — only when a court, statute, or agency record says so; quote and date it.

2 · Primary sources first

  • Prefer court dockets, ICC releases, statutes, and reputable reporting that cites them.
  • Screenshot culture is not a source — capture the URL and the dated headline.
  • ICC rows on this docket cite court allegations. Docket campaign counts are labeled not ICC — keep that honesty when you remix.

3 · FOIA / public records (20 minutes)

  • U.S. starter: foia.gov — how to file — institutional pressure, not private confrontation.
  • Ask for contracts, audits, and meeting minutes — not private addresses.
  • Log what you filed and what came back; that log is a network good.

4 · Corrections & counter-docket

  • Errors and counsel rebuttals go to the case Corrections / Counter-docket slots — strength, not weakness.
  • A retraction with a receipt is a win. Hiding history is not.

5 · What we never do

  • Violence, threats, stalking, doxxing, celebrating injury or death.
  • Fake warrants, agency seals, or law-enforcement poster chrome.
  • Recruiting tips to confront private persons.

6 · Chapter checklist (kitchen table)

  1. Read the charter aloud once.
  2. Pick one legal action from a case action kit (geo-aware).
  3. Ship one tiny good: translation line, FOIA template, or source list.
  4. Invite one person who is not already online and angry.
